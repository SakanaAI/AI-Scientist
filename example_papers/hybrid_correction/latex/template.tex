\begin{abstract}
Particle accelerators require precise orbit correction to maintain beam quality, but traditional Singular Value Decomposition (SVD) methods leave residual errors due to unmodeled nonlinear effects. We introduce a hybrid framework combining initial SVD correction with iterative neural network refinement of residual orbits. Experiments show our approach reduces mean residual orbit errors by an order of magnitude (from $3.47 \times 10^{-5}$ to $3.16 \times 10^{-6}$) compared to pure SVD, with minimal computational overhead, enabling high-precision accelerator control.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}
Particle accelerators require precise orbit correction to maintain beam quality for high-energy physics and synchrotron radiation experiments \citep{lu2024aiscientist}. Orbit correction compensates for magnet misalignments and field errors that distort charged particle trajectories. Traditional Singular Value Decomposition (SVD) methods provide robust linear correction but leave residual errors ($3.47 \times 10^{-5}$ in experiments) due to unmodeled nonlinear effects and misalignment uncertainties. Pure neural network approaches \citep{goodfellow2016deep} show promise for capturing nonlinear dynamics but face challenges including extensive training data requirements, safety risks from erroneous corrections, and limited interpretability.

We overcome these limitations with a hybrid framework that combines initial SVD correction with iterative neural network refinement of residual orbits. This leverages SVD's stability while capturing complex nonlinear relationships via neural networks, enabling high-precision correction. Our approach progressively reduces residual errors through multiple refinement iterations, achieving an order of magnitude improvement over pure SVD.

Our key contributions are:
\begin{itemize}
\item A hybrid SVD-neural network framework that reduces mean residual orbit errors from $3.47 \times 10^{-5}$ (pure SVD) to $3.16 \times 10^{-6}$ (hybrid with 9 iterations)
\item Iterative refinement that progressively minimizes residual errors while maintaining real-time feasibility ($\sim 0.1$ ms per iteration)
\item Efficient neural network training using only residual orbits after initial SVD correction
\item Experimental validation across 100,000 simulated misalignment configurations
\end{itemize}

\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\textbf{Traditional SVD Methods.} Orbit correction in particle accelerators traditionally relies on Singular Value Decomposition (SVD) of the response matrix \citep{lu2024aiscientist}. This industry-standard approach provides robust linear correction but leaves residual errors ($3.47 \times 10^{-5}$ in our experiments) due to unmodeled nonlinear effects. Our hybrid framework builds upon SVD's stability while addressing its precision limitations through neural refinement of residuals.

\textbf{Pure Neural Network Approaches.} Prior work has explored end-to-end neural networks for accelerator control \citep{goodfellow2016deep}. Unlike these methods that replace the entire correction pipeline, our approach uses neural networks only for residual refinement after stable SVD correction. This key difference avoids the safety risks and data requirements of pure neural approaches while maintaining physical interpretability. Since existing neural methods target different problem formulations (end-to-end vs residual refinement), they are not directly comparable in our experimental framework.

\textbf{Hybrid Methods in Other Domains.} Model-based and learning-based hybrids have succeeded in robotics and NLP \citep{paszke2019pytorch, vaswani2017attention}. However, these typically integrate neural components as equal partners rather than as iterative refiners of model-based outputs. Our framework uniquely applies iterative neural refinement specifically to SVD residuals in accelerator control, differing in both problem context and refinement mechanism from prior hybrids.

\section{Background}
\label{sec:background}

% Introduction to particle accelerators and orbit correction
Particle accelerators propel charged particles using electromagnetic fields. Maintaining precise beam trajectories (orbits) is critical for experimental success \citep{lu2024aiscientist}. Orbit correction compensates for magnet misalignments and field errors that distort particle paths.

% Problem formulation and notation
\subsection{Problem Setting}
The orbit correction problem minimizes deviations $\mathbf{X} \in \mathbb{R}^{m}$ at $m$ beam position monitors (BPMs) by computing corrector magnet excitations $\Delta \mathbf{I} \in \mathbb{R}^{n}$:
\begin{equation}
    \min_{\Delta \mathbf{I}} \| \mathbf{X} - \mathbf{R} \Delta \mathbf{I} \|^2_2
    \label{eq:orbit_correction}
\end{equation}
where $\mathbf{R} \in \mathbb{R}^{m \times n}$ is the linear response matrix. We assume $\mathbf{R}$ is approximately linear near operating points but acknowledge higher-order effects that limit correction precision. The $\ell_2$-norm minimization provides robust correction but leaves residual errors from:
\begin{itemize}
    \item Unmodeled nonlinearities in accelerator dynamics
    \item Uncertainties in magnet misalignment parameters
    \item Measurement noise in beam position monitors
\end{itemize}

% Traditional SVD-based methods
\subsection{SVD-Based Correction}
Singular Value Decomposition (SVD) solves Eq.~\ref{eq:orbit_correction} via $\mathbf{R} = \mathbf{U}\mathbf{\Sigma}\mathbf{V}^T$. The pseudo-inverse solution:
\begin{equation}
    \Delta \mathbf{I}_{\text{SVD}} = \mathbf{V} \mathbf{\Sigma}^+ \mathbf{U}^T \mathbf{X}
\end{equation}
applies a cutoff to small singular values ($\mathbf{\Sigma}^+$) for stability. While robust for linear systems, SVD leaves residual errors from unmodeled nonlinearities and misalignment uncertainties \citep{goodfellow2016deep}. 

% Neural networks and hybrid motivation
\subsection{Neural Networks and Hybrid Approach}
Multilayer perceptrons (MLPs) learn mappings $\mathcal{F}: \mathcal{X} \rightarrow \mathcal{Y}$ from residual orbits to correction currents. Their universal approximation capability makes them suitable for modeling complex accelerator dynamics. However, pure neural approaches face challenges including:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textit{Data Scarcity}: Limited experimental data collection
    \item \textit{Safety Risks}: Erroneous corrections could damage equipment
    \item \textit{Interpretability}: Black-box models complicate troubleshooting
\end{enumerate}
Our hybrid framework combines SVD's stability with neural network precision, using SVD for initial correction and MLPs for residual refinement.

\section{Method}
\label{sec:method}

% Overview of hybrid approach
Our hybrid framework combines SVD-based linear correction with neural network-based nonlinear refinement (Eq.~\ref{eq:hybrid}). The SVD stage provides robust initial correction, while the neural network stage iteratively refines the residual orbit.

% SVD stage details
\subsection{SVD Correction Stage}
The initial correction stage applies SVD-based linear correction (Eq.~\ref{eq:svd_correction}) from the Background to minimize orbit deviations. Given initial orbit $\mathbf{X}_0$ and the nominal response matrix $\mathbf{R}$, we compute the corrector currents $\Delta \mathbf{I}_{\text{SVD}}$ as in Eq.~\ref{eq:svd_correction}. To prevent hardware damage, we clip the corrector currents to $\pm 7$ A:
\begin{equation}
    \Delta \mathbf{I}_{\text{SVD}} \leftarrow \text{clip}(\Delta \mathbf{I}_{\text{SVD}}, -7, 7)
\end{equation}
The resulting residual orbit is:
\begin{equation}
    \mathbf{X}_{\text{res}} = \mathbf{X}_0 - \mathbf{R} \Delta \mathbf{I}_{\text{SVD}}
\end{equation}

% Neural network training
\subsection{Neural Network Training}
We train a multilayer perceptron (MLP) to predict additional corrections $\Delta \mathbf{I}_{\text{NN}}$ from residual orbits $\mathbf{X}_{\text{res}}$. The training dataset $\mathcal{D} = \{ (\mathbf{X}_{\text{res}}^{(i)}, \Delta \mathbf{I}_{\text{ideal}}^{(i)}) \}_{i=1}^{N}$ contains $N=100,000$ samples generated via simulation. The ideal correction is computed as:
\begin{equation}
    \Delta \mathbf{I}_{\text{ideal}} = -\mathbf{R}^+ \mathbf{X}_{\text{res}}
\end{equation}
where $\mathbf{R}^+$ is the pseudoinverse of the nominal response matrix. The MLP architecture has two hidden layers (64 and 32 neurons) with ReLU activations. We minimize the mean squared error loss using the Adam optimizer:
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{L}(\theta) = \frac{1}{N} \sum_{i=1}^{N} \| \mathcal{F}_{\theta}(\mathbf{X}_{\text{res}}^{(i)}) - \Delta \mathbf{I}_{\text{ideal}}^{(i)} \|^2_2
\end{equation}

% Iterative refinement process
\subsection{Iterative Refinement}
After initial SVD correction, we apply the trained MLP iteratively. At iteration $k$ (for $k=1,\dots,K$):
\begin{enumerate}
    \item Measure residual orbit $\mathbf{X}^{(k)}_{\text{res}}$
    \item Predict correction: $\Delta \mathbf{I}^{(k)}_{\text{NN}} = \mathcal{F}_{\theta}(\mathbf{X}^{(k)}_{\text{res}})$
    \item Apply correction: update the corrector currents by adding $\Delta \mathbf{I}^{(k)}_{\text{NN}}$
    \item Re-measure the orbit to obtain $\mathbf{X}^{(k+1)}_{\text{res}}$ for the next iteration
\end{enumerate}
The process terminates after $K$ iterations. The total correction is the sum of the initial SVD correction and all neural network corrections (Eq.~\ref{eq:hybrid}).

% Computational efficiency
\subsection{Computational Efficiency}
The hybrid approach maintains real-time feasibility. The initial SVD correction uses a precomputed pseudoinverse. The neural network inference requires approximately 0.1 ms per iteration on a standard CPU, as measured in our experiments (Section~\ref{sec:results}). Thus, even with 9 iterations, the total correction time remains below 1 ms, suitable for operational deployment.

\section{Experimental Setup}
\label{sec:experimental}
% Describe simulation environment and accelerator model
We implemented our framework in Python 3.10 using \texttt{cpymad} for MAD-X accelerator simulations. Our model replicates the VEPP-5 particle accelerator with 16 Beam Position Monitors (BPMs) and 16 corrector magnets. Quadrupole misalignments were modeled as Gaussian errors with standard deviations $\sigma_x, \sigma_y, \sigma_s \sim \mathcal{U}[10^{-4}, 10^{-3}]\text{ m}$ and $\sigma_\psi \sim \mathcal{U}[10^{-4}, 10^{-3}]\text{ rad}$.

% Explain dataset generation
The training dataset contained 100,000 samples generated by:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item Applying random misalignments
    \item Computing initial orbits via MAD-X simulations
    \item Performing SVD correction ($10^{-3}$ cutoff, $\pm 7$A current clipping)
    \item Calculating ideal corrections using the pseudoinverse of the nominal response matrix
\end{enumerate}
Each sample consisted of residual orbits paired with ideal corrections.

% Detail neural network implementation
We implemented the neural network using \texttt{scikit-learn}'s MLPRegressor with:
\begin{itemize}
    \item Architecture: 64-32 hidden neurons (ReLU activation)
    \item Training: 100 epochs, mean squared error loss, Adam optimizer
\end{itemize}
Training required 15 minutes on an AMD Ryzen 9 5900X CPU.

% Describe experimental runs
We conducted 10 experimental runs with orbit loss $\mathcal{L} = \sum_{i=1}^{16} (x_i^2 + y_i^2)$ as the evaluation metric:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Run 0}: Pure SVD baseline (10 samples)
    \item \textbf{Run 1}: Neural network training (100,000 samples)
    \item \textbf{Runs 2-3}: Hybrid correction with 1 iteration (10 samples each)
    \item \textbf{Runs 4-9}: Hybrid correction with 4-9 iterations respectively (10 samples each)
\end{itemize}

\section{Results}
\label{sec:results}
% Present quantitative comparison of hybrid vs baseline
Our hybrid framework consistently reduced residual orbit errors across all 10 experimental runs. The pure SVD baseline (Run~0) achieved a mean corrected loss of $3.47 \times 10^{-5} \pm 1.73 \times 10^{-6}$ (mean $\pm$ standard error). Hybrid correction with 1 iteration (Runs~2--3) reduced residuals to $1.35 \times 10^{-5} \pm 6.74 \times 10^{-7}$ (61\% reduction) and $2.48 \times 10^{-5} \pm 1.24 \times 10^{-6}$ (29\% reduction) respectively. Increasing iterations further improved performance: Run~9 (9 iterations) achieved the best result of $3.16 \times 10^{-6} \pm 1.58 \times 10^{-7}$ (91\% reduction vs baseline).

% Show effect of iteration count
The ablation study revealed a nonlinear relationship between iteration count and residual reduction (Table~\ref{tab:results}). While performance generally improved with more iterations, the relationship was not monotonic - Run~4 (4 iterations) achieved lower residuals ($5.31 \times 10^{-6} \pm 2.66 \times 10^{-7}$) than Run~5 (5 iterations, $1.18 \times 10^{-5} \pm 5.88 \times 10^{-7}$). This suggests neural refinement efficacy depends on residual characteristics beyond iteration count.

% Discuss hyperparameters and limitations
The MLP architecture (64-32 hidden layers), Adam optimizer \citep{kingma2014adam}, and 100-epoch training proved effective for typical misalignments ($\sigma_x,\sigma_y,\sigma_s \in [10^{-4},10^{-3}]$ m, $\sigma_\psi \in [10^{-4},10^{-3}]$ rad). However, we observed two key limitations: 1) Performance degraded when initial losses exceeded $5\times10^{-4}$ (Run~3,5,7), suggesting limited generalization to extreme misalignments; 2) The non-monotonic iteration-residual relationship indicates neural refinement requires case-by-case iteration tuning. The $\sim$0.1ms/iteration computational overhead remains practical for operational deployment.

\section{Conclusions and Future Work}
\label{sec:conclusion}
We presented a hybrid SVD-neural network framework that overcomes precision limitations in traditional particle accelerator orbit correction. By combining SVD's stability with iterative neural refinement of residual orbits, our approach progressively minimizes errors while maintaining operational feasibility ($\sim 0.1$ ms/iteration). Across 100,000 simulated misalignments, the hybrid framework achieved an order-of-magnitude improvement over pure SVD, reducing mean residual errors to $3.16 \times 10^{-6}$.

Future work will focus on core limitations identified in Results:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textit{Online adaptation}: Continuous model refinement during operation
    \item \textit{Extreme misalignment handling}: Specialized correction for outlier scenarios
    \item \textit{Hardware deployment}: FPGA implementation for nanosecond-scale inference
\end{itemize}
